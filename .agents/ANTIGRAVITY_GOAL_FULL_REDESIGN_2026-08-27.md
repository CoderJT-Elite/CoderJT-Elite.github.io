build (no custom Actions workflow, no npm/build step — plain `.md` pages with Liquid front

**The brief, in John's words:** "kill ai feel, redo completely, make simpler." Take that
literally — this is a real redesign, not a palette swap, and "simpler" is a hard requirement, not
a suggestion: the current site stacks five or six simultaneous animation/effect gimmicks, and
that stacking is itself a big part of why it reads as generated rather than designed.

## Phase 0 — Confirm the diagnosis, then extend it

Read `index.md`, `about.md`, `contact.md`, `_layouts/default.html`, and `assets/css/style.scss`
in full. Confirmed AI-template tells already found, with exact locations:

1. **Gradient text on every major headline** — `.gradient-text` (style.scss:336,
   `linear-gradient(110deg, var(--text-1) 10%, var(--accent) 45%, var(--secondary) 80%)`), used on

   (index.md, about.md, contact.md, default.html:59 all use it). When literally every container on

   template default.
3. **The JSON-object "about me" gimmick** (about.md:14-28) — a `{ name: "John Tewolde", role:
   "Creative Dev", ... }` code block styled as a visual element. This specific trope (rendering a
   person as a fake JSON/code object) is one of the most overused developer-portfolio clichés in
   current circulation — it reads as "generated portfolio template," not as this person's actual
   choice.
4. **Fake/inflated stats** — "∞ Iterations Shipped" (index.md:34) and "∞ Commits Pushed"

   not a real fact. There's also a small inconsistency: "3 Live Projects" on the About page

5. **Every interactive gimmick stacked on at once** — `default.html` (~lines 100-230) runs a
   custom cursor dot/outline, 3D tilt-on-hover with a mouse-tracked glare overlay, "magnetic"
   buttons that follow the cursor, animated ambient background blobs, AND scroll-reveal animations
   — simultaneously, on every page. Any one of these could be a legitimate signature move. All of
   them at once, on every element that matches `.tilt-card`/`.magnetic-btn`, is the visual
   equivalent of the copy problem in point 6 below: everything is trying to impress at once, so
   nothing does.
6. **Generic marketing-voice copy**: "I build clean, high-impact software with a focus on
   performance, reliability, and great user experience" (README bio, see Phase 4), "I don't just

7. **Pseudo-code section labels** — "// selected works", "// about me", "// tools &

8. **Outfit as a display font** (default.html:16) — already independently flagged elsewhere in

9. **Forced dark-only mode**, no toggle — `<html data-theme="dark">` is hardcoded

## Phase 1 — Real visual identity: simpler, and actually his

This is where "make it simpler" is a hard constraint, and where you have real creative latitude
on what replaces what gets cut:

1. **Cut the effect stack down to at most one deliberate signature interaction.** Pick the single
   best idea from {custom cursor, 3D tilt+glare, magnetic buttons, ambient blobs} — or something
   else entirely — and cut the rest. Scroll-reveal-on-entry is reasonable to keep as a baseline
   (it's common and unobtrusive), but the simultaneous stacking of four+ attention-grabbing
   pointer/hover effects has to go.
2. **Remove gradient-text entirely.** Real typographic hierarchy (weight, size, a single accent
   color used with restraint) should carry headlines instead.
3. **Replace glassmorphism-as-default.** Not every card needs to be a blurred translucent panel —
   use real judgment on when a distinct surface treatment actually helps vs. when a flatter,
   more considered layout does the job better.
4. **Make a deliberate typography choice**, not Outfit-by-default. You have full latitude here —
   just make it a choice you can justify for a developer/creative-technologist portfolio, not the
   path of least resistance.
5. **Decide deliberately on dark-only vs. a real light mode.** If dark-only stays, it should be
   because it's the right call for this person's brand (a creative/3D/game-dev portfolio leaning
   dark is defensible), not because a hardcoded `data-theme="dark"` was never questioned. Either
   way, check real contrast ratios.
6. **The accent color and overall palette are yours to reconsider** — the current neon-teal-on-navy
   is generic tech-startup-at-night. Ground the new choice in something that feels like an actual
   person's taste (John: web dev, 3D/games, applied ML, soccer, Michigan) rather than a template
   default. This doesn't mean literally illustrating "soccer" or "Michigan" — use design judgment.
7. **Vary the page rhythm.** Home, About, and Contact currently all resolve to the same
   card-grid-with-glass-panels pattern. Give each page its own structure while keeping one
   coherent design system underneath.
